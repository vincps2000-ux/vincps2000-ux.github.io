Came across Proebstings Law, a claim from Todd Proebsting, that claims a 18 Years performance doubling happens in Compiler Research.
This is contrasted to the 18-24 doubling claimed by Moore. (It was a bit of a Joke, implying compiler Research only for speed to be somewhat misplaced)

This is tested in this: (https://web.archive.org/web/20220603004137/http://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.29.434&rep=rep1&type=pdf)

The test is running code without and with Optimisations, and then working with the Speed-Up, assuming they were the reason of of Compiler optimisation.

Problems:
    - test Assumes the Speedup is evenly distributed across the years and not frontloaded.
    - Code might be optimized using other means.

A Speedup of 3.3 by Compiler is found(until 2001).

Later Looks at it Suggest that the Speedup is even less(https://oleksii.shmalko.com/20211028115609/). 

Around 1-2% speed up per year seems to be the common trend.


possible Intuitive Reasons for this:
    - smallest atomic unit of construction is closer to target Metric.
    - self-fufilling Prophecy: Speed up not main Goal on most applications.
    - Hard Limit: A certain amount of Computations F will be needed for every Programm P, to stay correct.
